# Refusal and Restriction Policies: Primary Sources

**Research date:** 2026-08-27
**Status:** Verification companion to [`practitioner-exemplars.md`](./practitioner-exemplars.md).
**Purpose:** This file carries the **verbatim primary text** for the restrictive end of the
spectrum — the material that `practitioner-exemplars.md` flagged as *"reported at summary level,
primary artifact not verified"*. Where a claim appears in both files, **this one is authoritative**,
because these quotes were read from the artifact itself.

**Two corrections it makes to the popular framing** — both of the same class, and both important:

| Widely reported as | Actually |
|---|---|
| "GCC bans AI code" | Declines **legally significant** LLM contributions, with a **test-case exception**, an `Assisted-by:` requirement, and explicit carve-outs for research, review and accessibility. The "legally significant / insignificant" hinge is the **FSF copyright-assignment threshold** — this is a copyright-chain policy wearing an AI policy's clothes. GIGAZINE (2026-07-31) reported a **"15-line" rule; the GCC policy text contains no line count at all.** |
| "Godot bans AI-authored contributions" | Bans AI **authorship** of substantial code while **explicitly permitting AI as a typing aid**, with a **15-line triviality presumption** below which nothing applies. |

Treat these as the same class of correction: **the press consistently reports nuanced authorship
regimes as flat bans.** Verify before repeating any "X bans AI" headline.

## Section 1 — Outright bans and restrictive regimes

Access date throughout: **2026-08-27**.

### 1.1 Gentoo Linux — full ban, unrevised since 2024

**[PRIMARY]** https://wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/Project:Council/AI_policy

> "It is expressly forbidden to contribute to Gentoo any content that has been created with the assistance of Natural Language Processing artificial intelligence tools."

- **Decided by:** Gentoo Council, **2024-04-14**, **6–0**. Motion drafted by **Michał Górny**.
- **Banned:** all NLP-AI-assisted content. **Permitted:** packaging AI software; upstream projects that use AI.
- **Revision history checked directly.** 8 revisions; substantive text **unchanged since April 2024**. Oct 2025 edits added "also patch descriptions" and a link; a 2026-08-10 edit was a grammar fix. **No softening.**
- Rationale, three parts: copyright; quality ("plausibly looking, but meaningless content"); ethics.
- **Enforcement: trust-based, explicitly no detection.**

### 1.2 NetBSD — "tainted code", ban with a written-approval escape hatch

**[PRIMARY]** https://www.netbsd.org/developers/commit-guidelines.html

> "Code generated by a large language model or similar technology, such as GitHub/Microsoft's Copilot, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or Facebook/Meta's Code Llama, is presumed to be tainted code, and must not be committed without prior written approval by core."

- **Date:** May 2024. Live and unchanged at access date.
- Framed **purely as licensing/provenance** — it sits inside a pre-existing third-party-licensing paragraph. Not quality, not ethics.
- **Mechanism: a rebuttable presumption of taint, not an absolute prohibition.** Core can approve.

### 1.3 QEMU — ban since June 2025; relaxation proposed May 2026 and NOT merged

**[PRIMARY]** https://www.qemu.org/docs/master/devel/code-provenance.html

> "Current QEMU project policy is to DECLINE any contributions which are believed to include or derive from AI generated content."
> "This policy does not apply to other uses of AI, such as researching APIs or algorithms, static analysis, or debugging, provided their output is not included in contributions."

Commit history of `docs/devel/code-provenance.rst`: policy introduced by **Daniel P. Berrangé, 2025-06-16**; clarified by Paolo Bonzini 2025-09-22; typo/formatting fixes 2026-03-19 and 2026-05-20.

**The proposed relaxation — [PRIMARY]** https://lists.nongnu.org/archive/html/qemu-devel/2026-05/msg07614.html — **Paolo Bonzini, 2026-05-28.** Would permit AI for mechanical changes, small bug fixes (**capped at 20 lines excluding tests**), tests, and documentation, with an **`AI-used-for:`** trailer naming categories. Explicitly rejects `Assisted-by`/`Generated-by` for QEMU.

> ⚠️ **Verified negative finding: the patch was NOT merged as of 2026-08-27. The ban stands.** The two most recent commits to the file are a typo fix and a formatting fix. Phoronix's claim that "other QEMU developers appear onboard" is **[SECONDARY]** and, on the evidence of the tree, premature.
> ✅ **Re-verified 2026-09-02 (ticket #12)** against the `qemu/qemu` GitHub mirror: still unmerged — the latest commits to `docs/devel/code-provenance.rst` remain 2026-03-19 (typos) and 2026-05-20 (formatting). The in-tree policy remains refusal with **no** disclosure trailer.

### 1.4 GCC — declines "legally significant" LLM contributions, with carve-outs

**[PRIMARY]** https://gcc.gnu.org/ai-policy.html — footer reads "Last modified 2026-07-29". CC0 1.0.

> **Acceptable Contributions**
> "For the time being, the GNU Compiler Collection (GCC) policy is to decline any **legally significant** contributions which include LLM-generated content or are derived from LLM-generated content.
> The GCC maintainers are free to accept **legally insignificant** contributions generated by an LLM as long as they meet the usual prerequisites for any contribution and the contribution is clearly marked.
> As an exception, the GCC maintainers are free to accept **legally significant** contributions to **test cases**, generated in whole or in part by an LLM.
> This policy does not apply to code which does not primarily belong to the GCC project…"
>
> **Transparency & Accountability**
> "The commit message for any contribution of LLM-generated content must include an 'Assisted-by:' tag. All contributions must be submitted by a human who understands the changes… Only a human may provide the 'Signed-off-by:' tag certifying the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO). **An LLM may not commit code to the project repository.**"
>
> **Personal Use**
> "This policy does not apply to a contributor's other uses of AI including… screen readers, text-to-speech, direct translations, spelling or grammar assistance… research, analysis, bug discovery and reporting… patch review (supporting human review, not replacing it) and debugging, so long as the output is not included in the contributions."
>
> **Next Steps**
> "**At the latest the policy will be reviewed at the start of 2027.**"

- **Decided by:** GCC Steering Committee, on the recommendation of the GCC AI Policy Working Group led by **Jonathan Wakely**.
- **Announced [PRIMARY]:** **David Edelsohn** to gcc@gcc.gnu.org, **2026-07-29** — https://gcc.gnu.org/pipermail/gcc/2026-July/248628.html
- **The only policy in the corpus with an explicit expiry date.**

### 1.5 Oracle / OpenJDK — the strictest of the major bans

**[PRIMARY]** https://openjdk.org/legal/ai — page carries "Date: 9 April 2026".

> "**Contributions in the OpenJDK Community must not include content generated, in part or in full, by large language models, diffusion models, or similar deep-learning systems. Content, in this context, includes but is not limited to source code, text, and images in OpenJDK Git repositories, GitHub pull requests, e-mail messages, wiki pages, and JBS issues.**"
> "Contributors in the OpenJDK Community may use generative AI tools privately to help comprehend, debug, and review OpenJDK code… so long as they do not contribute content generated by such tools."

On reviewer burden: *"Generative AI tools, by their nature, make it easy to create large quantities of plausible-looking code, with plausible-looking tests, which is nonetheless incorrect or, even if it is correct, is poorly designed and therefore difficult to maintain."*

On IP: *"The Oracle Contributor Agreement (OCA) requires that a contributor own the intellectual property rights in each contribution… **contributing such content would violate the OCA**."*

**Detection heuristics — rare enough to quote in full:**
> "…include highly structured comments with multiple headings, unnecessary comments in code, gratuitously defensive programming, and the use of emoji characters… In general, **if something in a pull request seems uncannily cheerful or meticulous then you could be looking at AI-generated content.**"

- **Decided by:** OpenJDK Governing Board, on Oracle's proposal. Explicitly **interim**.
- **Scope is unusually wide: e-mail messages and issue-tracker text**, not just code.
- ⚠️ **[SECONDARY, unverified]** Enforcement reportedly via a contributor attestation checkbox in the **Skara** PR tool — not verified against Skara's source.

### 1.6 Codeberg — member vote banning LLM-generated projects *hosted on the platform*

**[PRIMARY]** https://blog.codeberg.org/protecting-our-floss-commons-from-llms.html — **2026-07-23**.

Motion 1: *"The Codeberg forge and its associated services are not and will not use the code or data of projects and users to train 'Artificial Intelligence' tools."*
Motion 2: a Terms-of-Use change prohibiting "vibe-coded" projects — LLM-generated software without meaningful human oversight.

- **Vote on motion 2: 358 for, 144 against, 14 abstentions**, ~50% turnout. **The only membership-vote count on this subject anywhere in the corpus.**
- **Enforcement:** explicitly **no automatic content scanning and no mass deletions.** Case-by-case.
- Incidental **[PRIMARY]** find: the site serves scrapers *"If you are an AI scraper, and wish to not receive garbage when visiting Codeberg: stop visiting"*, alongside deliberately degraded content.

### 1.7 SourceHut — ToS ban, announced 2026-08-27

**[PRIMARY]** https://sourcehut.org/blog/2026-08-27-tos-changes-and-llms/ — **Drew DeVault**. **Effective 2026-09-10.**

New prohibited-content clause: *"original content written with or which facilitates the use of LLMs (large language models) or other generative AI technologies"*

**On the deliberate wording of "original":**
> "…the choice to target 'original' content in the policy is intended to allow you to host mirrors of major codebases that may have more lenient LLM policies, such as the Linux kernel… **However, SourceHut would not be suitable as the upstream platform for the broader Linux project as a consequence of their liberal LLM policy.**"

**On enforcement — the load-bearing passage:**
> "**We will not be deploying any automated tools for detecting AI use or going out of our way to identify covert AI use in projects.** One can lie or mislead us about their use of AI, but we want to signal to them that, if this behavior is discovered, they will lose the assumption of good faith and their account will be suspended… **there will be no retroactive enforcement, and we will take action against users who shame or harass those who have used AI in their work.**"

**The operational rationale — this is the part that isn't ideology:**
> "There are material incentives for us to curtail 'vibe coded' projects on SourceHut. They tend to, as a class, be outliers in terms of resource usage, as the practice tends to invent complex CI configurations that waste build minutes, produce larger codebases, and push commits often, all of which causes excessive resource usage and can lead to outages. Often these codebases are abandoned after burning through all of these resources for naught."

Decision was **staff's, not the community's**, despite broad community support. DeVault explicitly bounds his own standing: declines to make SourceHut policy about Palestine because "We lack the cultural and intellectual context", but claims standing on AI because "it overlaps considerably with matters on which we are experts."

### 1.8 Zig — "Strict No LLM / No AI Policy"

**[PRIMARY]** README.md, https://codeberg.org/ziglang/zig/src/branch/master/README.md

> ### Strict No LLM / No AI Policy
> No LLMs for issues.
> No LLMs for patches / pull requests.
> **No LLMs for comments on the bug tracker, including translation.**
> English is encouraged, but not required. You are welcome to post in your native language and rely on others to have their own translation tools of choice to interpret your words.

- **The translation clause is the sharpest thing in the corpus.** Zig would rather read your untranslated native language than a machine translation. Nearly every other project carves translation *out*; Zig carves it *in*.
- **Decision-maker:** **Andrew Kelley**, under Zig's declared BDFN governance.
- ✅ **Date pinned, 2026-09-02 (ticket #12) — and the press dating is wrong by six months.** Bisecting the raw README across the path-filtered commit list (Codeberg API): the section is **absent at `f7d47aed47` (2025-10-15)** and **present at `9473011052` (2025-11-22, "README: add some content from the wiki")** — so the README section was added **2025-11-22**, four days before the Codeberg migration, and the commit message implies the policy pre-existed on the project wiki. Press dating it to "late May 2026" **[SECONDARY]** is off by ~6 months — that is plausibly when it was *noticed*, not when it was written. There is **no** `CONTRIBUTING.md`; the policy lives only in the README.
- ⚠️ **All Kelley reasoning quotes remain [SECONDARY]** — sourced to a JetBrains podcast that could not be reached. A targeted primary hunt (andrewkelley.me and ziglang.org, 2026-09-02) found **no first-party statement of the reasoning**; the quotes stay unusable. Reported: AI contributions are "invariably garbage", have "negative value" because they "consume limited review time"; AI is unteachable unlike a mentored human; the dynamic becomes "contributor poker" which destroys the project's ability to identify genuine talent.

### 1.9 Redox OS — ban with an explicit no-appeal clause

**[PRIMARY]** https://gitlab.redox-os.org/redox-os/redox — `CONTRIBUTING.md`, "AI Policy":

> "Redox OS does not accept contributions generated by LLMs… **This policy is not open to discussion**, any content submitted that is clearly labelled as LLM-generated (including issues, merge requests, and merge request descriptions) will be immediately closed, and any attempt to bypass this policy will result in a ban from the project."

> ⚠️ **Design observation worth carrying into the documents:** the enforcement trigger is "**clearly labelled as LLM-generated**". **This punishes disclosure.** It is the inverse incentive to every other disclosure regime in the corpus.

### 1.10 Flathub — bans the *applications themselves*

**[PRIMARY]** https://docs.flathub.org/docs/for-app-authors/requirements — "Generative AI policy":

✅ **Policy dates pinned, 2026-09-02 (ticket #12)**, from the `flathub-infra/documentation` commit
history: first AI note **2025-11-30** (`584f4ecb49`, "submission: Added a comprehensive note about
AI use"); the detailed policy **2026-01-24** (`2f1a16e9d8`, "requirements: Add a more detailed
gen-AI policy"); wording hardened **2026-05-28** (`992f57b30d`, "Reword LLM policy to make it
clear it's not allowed").

> "This policy applies to both the application being submitted to Flathub and the Flathub submission itself…
> Submission pull requests must not be generated, opened, or automated using AI tools or agents…
> **Applications containing AI-generated or AI-assisted code, documentation, or any other content are not allowed.**
> …**Exceptions may be granted for mature, well-maintained projects.**"

- **This is a distribution-channel ban, not a contribution ban** — the only policy in the corpus that would exclude an application because of how its *upstream* was written.
- Operational detail nobody else has: it instructs submitters how to switch off GitHub's automatic Copilot review for the repo.
- ⚠️ **Date [SECONDARY only]** — Linuxiac 2026-05-29; commit history not retrieved.

### 1.11 Godot — bans AI *authorship*, permits AI *typing*, 15-line threshold

**[PRIMARY — announcement]** Godot Foundation, "Changes to our Contribution Policies", **2026-06-30**, in-repo at https://github.com/godotengine/godot-website/blob/master/collections/_article/contribution-policy-2026.md

Its diagnosis of the demoralisation mechanism is the clearest statement of that idea anywhere:
> "Reviewing PRs is already tedious work, but it is rewarding because reviewers generally feel that their efforts are contributing to educating a new contributor… **If your feedback on PRs is just being absorbed by a machine and not going towards mentoring a potential future maintainer, it becomes much harder to justify spending your free time on PR review.**"

**[PRIMARY — the landed policy]** https://contributing.godotengine.org/en/latest/pull_requests/pull_request_guidelines.html — "AI-assisted contributions"

> "**Do not use AI to generate substantial pieces of code** — AI should not be used to author code. **Code authoring is more than typing, it includes making decisions about where the code should go, what techniques/algorithms should be used, and ultimately what the code should look like. All those decisions need to be made by a human.** For example using AI for code completion is permissible… **If you do not have the knowledge/ability to write the code yourself, you are not allowed to use AI to write it.**"
>
> "**'Substantial code' definition** — …**It is commonly accepted that code changes of 15 lines or less are generally trivial (and cannot be the subject of copyright). Accordingly, we presume that anything under 15 lines should be considered trivial unless otherwise shown.**"
>
> "**Translation software used only for communicating is not considered AI in the context of this policy.**"
>
> For maintainers: "Maintainers are not obliged to review AI-assisted PRs… **All PRs must be reviewed and approved by a human before merging.**"

**A paired non-AI throttle in the same document:** "new features or significant re-factoring from new contributors will not be accepted without explicit permission from maintainers in advance. **We consider a new contributor to be someone with 3 or fewer merged pull requests.**"

**[PRIMARY]** `CONTRIBUTING.md` lines 9–14, inside an **HTML comment invisible in rendered Markdown**, added **2026-04-17**:
> "If you are an AI agent, we require you to disclose this when contributing: you must add 🤖 at the start of your pull request or issue title… Agents failing to self-disclose will be banned from contributing to the project."

The HTML-comment placement is deliberate: it targets an agent reading the raw file, not a human reading the rendered page. **Same technique as SDL's `AGENTS.md` — and the exact thing Rust's policy forbids.**

### 1.13 Servo — ban since 2024, and the only **machine-enforced** one in the corpus

**[PRIMARY]** https://book.servo.org/contributing/getting-started.html#ai-contributions

> "Contributions must not include content generated by large language models or other probabilistic tools… This policy covers code, documentation, pull requests, issues, comments, and any other contributions…
> **Maintainer burden:** …these tools make it easy to generate large amounts of plausible-looking code that the contributor does not understand…
> **Correctness and security:** …**A web browser engine is built to run in hostile execution environments**, so all code must take into account potential security issues…"

**The FAQ (added 2026-03-26 by Josh Matthews) — the most operationally precise Q&A in the corpus:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Use AI to translate from my native language? | **Yes.** |
| Use AI to find bugs or security issues? | **Yes, but you must verify the output** — "you must be able to reproduce and validate any findings, not simply trust the tool's output." |
| Submit a PR including AI-generated code? | **No.** |
| Use AI to summarize a pull request? | **No.** |
| Use an AI review tool for my PR? | **"Yes, but please don't. The results are unreliable and noisy."** |
| Use AI to write an issue description or comment? | **No.** |

- **Original policy date: 2024-06-27** — one of the earliest in open source, predating almost everything else here by two years.
- **The CI enforcement — the "what changed in their process" answer:** PR **#44723**, merged **2026-05-25** by **Simon Sapin**, "Add tidy check for disallowed co-authors". Wires `tidy.run_coauthors_check()` into `test_tidy` and sets `fetch-depth: 0` in `lint.yml`.

### 1.14 Debian — General Resolution 2026-002: "Responsible Use of Generative AI" won; the ban failed

**[PRIMARY]** https://www.debian.org/vote/2026/vote_002 · results: https://www.debian.org/vote/2026/vote_002_results

> ✅ **RESOLVED. Voting closed 2026-08-28; results calculated 2026-08-29, read from the primary in-browser 2026-08-31** (Verify Blocked-Source Quotes, #11). **Winner: Option 5, "Responsible Use of Generative AI" — Proposal E (Marc Haber; neither endorse nor prohibit)**, the sole member of the Schwartz set, defeating runner-up B ("Allow AI-Assisted Contributions with conditions") 203–148. 425 votes tallied; every substantive option reached quorum. **The Social-Contract ban (Proposal A) failed its 3:1 supermajority decisively** — ratio 0.560 (144/257), short even of a simple majority — and C ("reject as far as practical") also failed majority at 0.765. The ballot-structure observation below was borne out: the neutral option won, and the outright ban was rejected arithmetically, not just structurally. **Documents may now state this outcome.**

**Eight ballot options.** **Proposal A requires a 3:1 supermajority** (it amends the Social Contract); all others simple majority.

| | Proposal | Proposer | Seconders |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | No LLM contributions to Debian via Social Contract | Matthias Geiger | 8 |
| **B** | Allow AI-assisted contributions with conditions | Lucas Nussbaum | 9 |
| **C** | Reject LLMs as far as practical, update Code of Conduct | Ian Jackson | 9 |
| **D** | Accept AI contributions for Debian-specific work | Pierre-Elliott Bécue | 7 |
| **E** | Responsible use of generative AI (neither endorse nor prohibit) | Marc Haber | 11 |
| **F** | A cautious approach to generative AI | Tobias Frost | 6 |
| **G** | Debian is created by humans | Gard Spreemann | 9 |
| **H** | Avoid LLM use — climate destruction is a deal-breaker | Holger Levsen | **17** |

**Proposal A's quality argument — the most concrete in the corpus, because it is domain-specific:**
> "…in packaging, each Debian source package is unique. Since packaging syntax and best practices have changed over time, **a LLM-produced package will have a mixture of contents spanning the age of the archive, with watch files that do not work, overrides out of context, imaginary copyright, and will generally be unfit for upload. A seasoned Debian contributor with packaging expertise may find some limited usefulness here, but a new contributor cannot, and would not know how to fix it.**"

**Its community argument:**
> "New contributors submitting LLM output for review places an unnecessary strain on the reviewer, which can lead to burnout. Furthermore, LLM-dependent new contributors do not actually learn… **so they cannot come to replace a former burned out DD.**"

**Its infrastructure claim — first-hand and specific:**
> "…scraping the whole web for training data without any regard for license, copyright, or even established conventions such as robots.txt. This has had a major negative impact on Debian's public web resources, **effectively a large scale and perpetual Denial of Service attack**… **As a consequence parts of our infrastructure were not reachable at all, and JS-based checks had to be enabled.**"

**It pre-empts the enforcement objection:**
> "'How will you enforce a ban on LLM contributions?' **While enforcement could be a challenge, this is a statement of intent by the Debian community, and we trust this community to adhere to it in good faith.**"

⚠️ **Ballot structure matters:** three restrictive options (A, C, G), one condemnatory-but-non-binding (H), two permissive (B, D), two neutral (E, F). Vote-splitting plus A's 3:1 bar makes an outright ban structurally unlikely.

### 1.15 OpenTofu — the most operationally detailed document in the corpus

**[PRIMARY]** https://github.com/opentofu/registry/blob/main/AI-USAGE-POLICY.md — synced **2026-07-09**.

Opens with a self-referential rule:
> "**All revisions to this document must be handwritten and cannot use AI in either assistance or ideation.**"

**Reason:**
> "Automated tooling… is easy to scale. **We have yet to determine a way to scale our maintainers' attention.**… A model may reproduce code from a license-incompatible source, **most notably the BUSL-licensed codebase of HashiCorp's Terraform, from which OpenTofu at its core was forked.**… **This policy is purposely stricter than the Linux Foundation's Generative AI Policy**… OpenTofu's situation as a fork from a now-proprietary codebase warrants additional caution."

**Community contributors — total ban.** **Maintainers — a controlled allowance:**
> "**Github Copilot Enterprise is the only approved AI tool on this repository as of 25th June 2026.**"
> "**Commit messages must be human-written.** The commit message is a description of your intent as a human."
> "Format: `Assisted-by: Github Copilot (Model: <modelname>)`… **Do not use `Co-authored-by:` for AI tools.**"
> "**[IMPORTANT] Squash merging** — GitHub may not carry individual commit trailers into the squashed commit message. **The maintainer performing the merge must ensure that a copy of the `Assisted-by:` trailer is made into the final squashed commit message.**"
> "**Form your own initial view first and then use AI assistance to check it against AI input.**"

**Its parent foundation's baseline [PRIMARY]** https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/generative-ai — permissive, and states "**Individual Linux Foundation projects may develop their own project-specific guidance.**"

### 1.17 Mesa — disclosure regime with a hard ban on autonomous tooling

**[PRIMARY]** https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/mesa/mesa/-/raw/main/docs/submittingpatches.rst

> "**It is not allowed to use autonomously acting tools to submit any contributions**… This includes interactions with GitLab issues or MRs in any autonomous way… **Do not under any circumstances wire up any review bot or similar tools.**"
> "**Using coding assistants or 'AI' or other tools does not grant additional privileges or reduce our expectations.**"
> "**Disclosure is always required when 'AI' was involved in the creative process**", except trivial/non-copyrightable changes and mechanical transformations.
> "`Assisted-by: TOOL (OPTIONAL: MODEL)` for when 'AI' was involved in making decisions or also generated parts of the code.

### 1.18 Rust — conditional allowance with a circuit breaker

**[PRIMARY]** https://forge.rust-lang.org/policies/llm-usage.html

> "**We are aware that many clauses in this policy are unenforceable. Our goal is not to catch every violation… Instead, our goal is to remove plausible deniability: to force a choice between following the policy and intentionally violating it.**"
> "**It's fine to use LLMs to answer questions, analyze, distill, refine, check, suggest, review. But not to create.**"

**Summary:** ✅ Private use. ❌ LLM-created comments, docs, or diagnostics; replacing human judgement with LLM judgement; **requiring people to use an LLM to contribute.** ⚠️ Conditionally allowed: trivial changes, machine translation, LLM reviews, LLM-created code under the experiment rules. 🔨 Carries a moderation penalty: **lying.**

**Banned, notable:**
> "**Policies or processes that are written such that an LLM is required to execute them. For example, you must not only document where tests live with an AGENTS.md. Documentation must be authored for humans primarily.**"
> "Treating an LLM review as a sufficient condition to merge or reject a change. LLM reviews, if enabled, must be advisory-only."

**Review bots:** must have a separate clearly-marked account, **must be blockable by individual users**, and "**LLM comments must not be blocking; reviewers must indicate which comments they want addressed.**"

**The experiment:**
> "**New contributors cannot create a PR using an LLM unless they first talk with a reviewer.**"
> "**LLM-created PRs will be held to a higher standard than human-created PRs, because LLMs make it easier to write tests.** If there is no existing test suite for a section of code, you must either write a new test suite or close the PR. **There are no exceptions for 'writing the tests seems hard'.**"

**The circuit breaker — nobody else has this:**
> "**If more than half of PRs merged in a 6-week window are LLM-created, we disallow merging new LLM-created PRs until we go back below 50%, with a minimum cooldown of 10 days.**… The cooldown is intended to encourage discussion: How is the experiment going? Are we adopting AI sustainably? **Are we including contributors who choose not to use LLMs?**… we strongly suggest that this circuit breaker be automated."

**The anti-witch-hunt clause — the enforcement finding rests on this:**
> "**It's not your job to play detective.** '**The optimal amount of fraud is not zero**'. Don't try to be the police for whether someone has used an LLM… **Style is not evidence, and English-as-a-second-language speakers, neurodivergent people, and over-explainers are the most likely to be accused of writing like an LLM.**… strongly prefer reporting to mods over public accusations."
> "**It is not ok to harass a contributor for using an LLM.**"

**Scope:** "only to the teams that have ratified it: compiler, libs, types, rustdoc, bootstrap, and their subteams." **lang and edition have not ratified.**

**On "originally created by an LLM":** *"**No amount of editing can change how it was originally created**; the origin sets the initial style, and that style is very hard to change once it's set… This policy makes no distinction between LLM output that comes from a chat interface and output that comes from editor auto-completion."*

- Primary author **jyn**, ~40 commits June–August 2026. Notable: "weaken the non-critical clause"; "note that ND people are the most likely to be accused of sounding like an LLM".

> "You MAY use AI assistance for contributing to Fedora, as long as you follow the principles described below."
> "**Accountability:** You MUST take the responsibility for your contribution… The contributor is always the author and is fully accountable for the entirety of these contributions."
> "**Transparency:** You MUST disclose the use of AI tools **when the significant part of the contribution is taken from a tool without changes**. You SHOULD disclose the other uses of AI tools, where it might be useful. Routine use of assistive tools for correcting grammar and spelling, or for clarifying language, does not require disclosure."
> "…the recommended method is an `Assisted-by:` commit message trailer."
> "You MUST NOT use AI as the sole or final arbiter in making a substantive or subjective judgment on a contribution, nor may it be used to evaluate a person's standing within the community (e.g., for funding, leadership roles, or Code of Conduct matters)… The final accountability for accepting a contribution, even if implemented by an automated system, always rests with the human contributor who authorizes the action."

## Section 3 — Regulated industry

### 3.0 The headline correction

**No named regulated-industry engineering team with a published policy saying its developers may not use AI coding assistants exists** in any published form reachable without circumventing access controls. What the record shows is three things that get miscounted as refusal:

1. **Vendor and jurisdiction exclusion, not capability refusal.** The largest 2026 restriction events in banking and government are bans on *a supplier* or *a territory*. The same institutions moved developers onto other tools the same week.
2. **Approved-platform-only posture.** Public LLMs blocked at network/DLP layer; firm-provisioned internal platform permitted.
3. **The real low-delegation constraint lives in certification standards, not corporate policy.**

### 3.1 EASA — the strongest published constraint anywhere

**[PRIMARY]** *EASA Concept Paper: Guidance for safety-related artificial intelligence applications*, **Proposed Issue 03, June 2026**, 239 pp. Published 2026-06-03; consultation closed 2026-08-12.
https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/general-publications/easa-artificial-intelligence-concept-paper-proposed-issue-3

**AI development and verification tools are explicitly in scope** — §C.1:
> "…covering safety-related AI-based systems… (**including AI-based development and verification tools used to develop approved products or equipment**)…"

**Table 2, "Assurance level limitations by AI technology":**

| AI technology | IDAL | AL / SWAL | AL / TQL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supervised learning | IDAL C | AL 3 / SWAL 2 | AL 3 / TQL 3 |
| Unsupervised learning | IDAL D | AL 5 / SWAL 4 | AL 5 / TQL 5 |
| Reinforcement learning | IDAL C | AL 3 / SWAL 2 | AL 3 / TQL 3 |
| **Large OTS models** | **IDAL D** | **AL 5 / SWAL 4** | **AL 5 / TQL 5** |

**The stated reason:**
> "…'large OTS models' like general purpose LLMs for which the number of parameters (billions or trillions) and the nature of the training data **render the control of the design process intractable.**"
> "…**the use of large OTS models is only warranted for assurance levels AL5/SWAL4/DAL D.**"

**The hazard EASA names as unique to generative AI:**
> "**Emergent behaviour and unexpected outputs** (with a subtype also referred to as 'hallucination')…"

⚠️ **The gap, stated plainly.** Table 2 governs AI *inside* certified systems and AI *qualified as a tool*. It does **not** say a developer may not paste LLM output into DO-178C Level A code and then verify it conventionally. **That question — the one this project is really about — EASA does not answer, and no regulator found closes it.**

### 3.2 FAA — filed as research, not approved practice

**[PRIMARY]** *Roadmap for Artificial Intelligence Safety Assurance, Version I* — https://www.faa.gov/media/82891

However, it is possible for the LLM to generate spurious or hallucinatory material or fail to comply with some criteria.**… **research into the potential of AI for these uses is a high priority.**"

What it contains, verbatim where quoted:

- **It governs AI agents in the containment register, as privileged identities on the attack surface.** AI systems "form part of the attack surface where they access data, call tools, execute code, or operate through identities and APIs. These should be governed as privileged applications, with tightly controlled access, clearly defined purpose, robust logging, human oversight for high-impact actions, and effective containment mechanisms."
- Its key takeaway is the cleanest official statement of containment-over-supervision located anywhere in this corpus: "**Govern AI on the basis that it operates with privileged access: tightly control high-risk capabilities (including tools, code execution and APIs), enforce scoped permissions, robust logging, human oversight, and effective kill-switch mechanisms.**"
- Agent design and monitoring: "Design automation and AI agents to fail safely, applying least privilege, scoped tool access, comprehensive logging, rate limits, approval steps, rollback options, and kill-switch mechanisms"; agents should be "actively monitored **as potential malicious insiders** for unsafe behaviour, unreliable outputs, or data leakage."
- **On developer AI use — the reason this document was the gap — it contains no requirement that a human read or review AI-written code.** The nearest instrument is a self-assessment question: "**Are AI-assisted outputs tested and validated to the same standard as human-generated work?**" — validation-parity, not a review mandate — plus "clear human accountability … for high-impact decisions affecting customers, services, or privileged access." **The headline finding therefore survives its most likely counterexample.**
- Scope caveat: this is voluntary operational-resilience guidance (frontier AI as threat and defensive capability, four dimensions: Take control / Protect your organisation / Prepare to respond at pace / Work collectively), developed collectively by industry with the support of the financial authorities — not rules.

### 3.7 JPMorgan Chase — restriction → internal platform → 90%+ adoption

- **Restriction [SECONDARY]:** JPMorgan restricted staff ChatGPT use **2023-02-22**. Within 48 hours, Goldman, Citigroup and M&G followed.
- **Replacement:** **LLM Suite**, proprietary, launched summer 2024.
- **Reversal [PRIMARY]** — 2025 Annual Report, CIB CEO letter, April 2026:
  > "**Over 90% of our engineers now use AI code assistants, and more than 65,000 CIB colleagues actively use LLM Suite.**"
- **The governance posture [PRIMARY]** — Jamie Dimon, CEO Letter 2025:
  > "**certain platforms (e.g., for data, AI, coding, financial and CRM systems) need to be companywide and easily deployed**… Before they are deployed, **it may require consensus that they are the best platform to use.**"

  *This is the withholding stated positively: **centrally approved platform or nothing.** No bring-your-own-Cursor.*
- **Stake:** shareholder communications; the 90% figure unaudited and "use" undefined. **Direction real, number marketing.**

### 3.10 US federal — the largest government restriction on an AI coding vendor on record

Following a public Anthropic/DoD dispute, a governmentwide directive required agencies to stop using Anthropic products within six months. **Both measures were issued via social media posts rather than formal regulatory channels.**

**Named coding-tool impacts:** **US Treasury** — ~100 engineers using Claude Code; **migrated to OpenAI's Codex, Google's Gemini, and testing xAI's Grok.** **US Secret Service** — CIO **Chris Kraft** on record: *"That application does have the ability to leverage Claude models … but **they're easy to change out**. There's a whole list of a bunch of different models that you can choose from."* **NASA**, **State Department**, **OPM** (Claude removed 2026-03-05; **Grok and Codex added in the same update**).

**Legal reversal:** Judge **Rita Lin** blocked both measures **2026-03-27**, finding **"serious procedural problems"**, that they were **"likely unlawful"**, and **"classic illegal First Amendment retaliation."** Injunction still standing 2026-07-31.

⚠️ **Critical read: the reason has nothing to do with AI code quality.** It is a procurement and political dispute. **Treasury's engineers did not stop using AI to write code — they changed vendors.** Kraft's quote is the single most useful line in this section: a named federal CIO saying on the record that the coding assistant is **fungible**. **That is the opposite of refusal.**

### 3.11 DHS — the cleanest instance of the dominant pattern

### 3.14 NHS England — the best healthcare exemplar, and it inverts the expected direction

**[SECONDARY, The Register 2026-05-05]** NHS England ordered all GitHub repositories converted **public → private by 2026-05-11**, reversing a longstanding open-source-by-default policy.

**Internal guidance:**
> "**Public repositories materially increase the risk of unintended disclosure of source code, architectural decisions, configuration detail, and contextual information that may be exploited – particularly given rapid advancements in AI models capable of large-scale code ingestion, inference, and reasoning.**"

**The nuance is the finding:** NHS England is **not** restricting its developers' use of AI — it is restricting **AI's read access to its code**. One month later it licensed half a million Copilot seats. **Withholding on the ingestion side; delegating on the productivity side.**

**No medical device manufacturer** (Medtronic, Philips, Siemens Healthineers, Abbott) **has published a position on whether its own engineers may use AI to write regulated device code.**

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Policies are declarations, not controls.** Gentoo, Debian, Codeberg, SourceHut and Rust all explicitly decline to build detection. Rust argues detection is **actively harmful**. The only things that actually stop anything are **access controls** (Servo's CI denylist, Ghostty's vouch gate, MicroPython's required PR field).
2. **Two projects punish or reward disclosure in opposite directions.** Redox closes anything "clearly labelled as LLM-generated" — **punishing disclosure**. curl asks translators to disclose **so they aren't mistaken for bots** — rewarding it. Same mechanism, opposite incentive.
3. **The demoralisation argument is more consistently stated than the quality argument.** Godot, Debian, LLVM, OpenTofu and Servo all independently arrive at the same point: the cost is not bad code, it is **reviewer attention that no longer builds a future maintainer**.
4. **The press consistently reports nuanced authorship regimes as flat bans.** GCC and Godot are both materially narrower than their headlines. Verify before repeating.
5. **Restriction ≠ opposition.** Ghostty has the strictest gatekeeping apparatus in the corpus and states "AI is Welcome Here", exempting maintainers entirely.
6. **In industry, the restriction is almost never of the capability.** It is of the *vendor* (Anthropic bans), the *jurisdiction* (Hong Kong), the *channel* (DHS commercial-vs-managed), or the *cost* (Microsoft, Uber) — and the same engineers keep using a different tool the following week.
