Committer

The team's ceiling lets model-generated changes of a defined class reach trunk unread. A human still gates the release.

Vocabulary as of 2026-08-31 Taxonomy: ADR-0004 Sources last verified 2026-08-31

Kinds, not ranks: no archetype is the goal state, and moving further along the spectrum means more engineered verification, not less discipline. Human oversight varies independently within every archetype.

01What this archetype is

A Committer team's configured ceiling permits model-generated changes of a defined class to merge to trunk with no human reading them. The archetype names the ceiling — what the settings permit — not what any individual change receives: most changes on a Committer team may still be human-reviewed, and the auto-merged class is deliberately narrow. Spotify draws exactly this line, auto-merging deterministic transforms while human-reviewing every LLM PRSpotify, Jun 2026.

02The boundary crossed

What separates Committer from Contributor is merge rights: crossing this boundary, the agent's output reaches trunk with no human action in between. The boundary is a discontinuity, not a matter of degree — a credential the previous archetype structurally lacks. On GitHub it must be built deliberately (a bot account, a bespoke workflow, a ruleset bypass), because the platform forbids its own agent from merging its own pull requestsGitHub Docs, Aug 2026.

03What a human still gates

Reach fixes the archetype; oversight varies within it. On a Committer team a human still gates the release train, and typically also gates which change classes qualify for unread merge. State what is gated specifically — the independence rule forbids implying that further along the spectrum means less supervised.

Independence rule

Containment and human oversight move independently. This section names the oversight; the spectrum position above names only the reach.

04Who works this way

Named practitioners with their stake declared — and an honest occupancy statement where the evidence is thin. The genuine agent-authored cases of unreviewed merge as stated policy number three, and every one is a narrow carve-outPractitioner Exemplars, Aug 2026.

05What it gains you, what it costs

What it gains you
  • Review attention concentrates on changes a human can actually help.
  • Mechanically-checkable classes stop queueing behind human availability.
What it costs you
  • The qualifying line must be engineered, argued for, and policed.
  • Verification machinery becomes load-bearing the moment no one reads the diff.

06What it demands of verification

The entailment: whatever a human stopped doing must be replaced by engineered verification — and the demand is written as a requirement, never as a claim that teams meet it. The machinery exists but is almost never wired to autonomyVerification Infrastructure, Aug 2026.

07Governance implications

What the settings mean contractually and legally at this ceiling — liability keyed to the setting, authorship versus adoption, attribution survival. Nothing in law or standards requires a human to read source code, so compliance is not what stops teams hereGovernance, Legal & IP, Aug 2026.

08The posture that fits

This fits a team that believes trust should be earned by change class rather than by author — and that where a class can be checked mechanically, a human reading it adds cost without adding safety.

The safety belief underneath: the class is mechanically checkable. The control moves; it does not improve.
