The spectrum

Five archetypes, ordered by reach

An archetype is a team's configured ceiling for model-generated changes. Each boundary is a capability the previous archetype structurally lacks. Click an archetype to see what it is made of.

Kinds, not ranks: no archetype is the goal state. Moving further along the spectrum means replacing human-in-the-loop verification with engineered verification — more rigor, not less. Human oversight varies independently within every archetype.

▸Autocomplete reaches the open file
Made of
no tool surface — the model proposes into the open file and a human accepts every edit
Demands first
nothing new — the existing review process carries all the weight it always did
What it gains you
  • Every line is read by the person shipping it.
  • No new verification or governance surface at all.
What it costs you
  • Human attention is the throughput ceiling.
  • None of the review burden is relieved.

Fits a team that believes reading every line is non-negotiable, and that anything reducing what a human reads costs more than it saves.

▸Workspace reaches the working tree
Made of
file-write and shell tool surfaces, no push credential — the agent acts across the working tree, locally
Demands first
containment: a sandbox or container, an egress allowlist, least-privilege tool scopes
What it gains you
  • Real multi-file work happens without a human approving each step.
  • Blast radius is bounded by the machine, whoever is watching.
What it costs you
  • Containment becomes load-bearing and must be engineered.
  • The review burden arrives all at once, at push time.

Fits a team that believes risk is bounded by what the agent can reach, not by who is watching it.

▸Contributor reaches the shared repository
Made of
a push credential — the agent opens pull requests; a human merges every one
Demands first
review hygiene at volume: queue visibility, and honesty about rubber-stamping as approvals accumulate
What it gains you
  • The agent works end to end; everything still passes a human gate.
  • Agent work comes through the same front door as anyone else's.
What it costs you
  • The review queue becomes the bottleneck.
  • Reviewer attention degrades exactly as volume grows.

Fits a team that believes review is the load-bearing control and should stay human — and that an agent's work should come through the same front door as anyone else's.

▸Committer reaches trunk, unread
Made of
merge rights for a defined change class — that class reaches trunk with no human reading it; a human gates release
Demands first
the qualifying gate itself: a mechanical definition of which changes may merge unread, and revert machinery for when it is wrong
What it gains you
  • Mechanically-checkable classes stop queueing behind humans.
  • Review attention concentrates where a human can actually help.
What it costs you
  • The qualifying line must be engineered, argued for, and policed.
  • Verification is load-bearing the moment no one reads the diff.

Fits a team that believes trust should be earned by change class rather than by author — and that where a class can be checked mechanically, a human reading it adds cost without adding safety.

▸Operator reaches running production
Made of
a pre-authorised path to production — nothing human stands between the agent's change and running prod, whether by CD-from-trunk or agent-held deploy credentials
Demands first
the full engineered chain: gates, canaries, auto-revert, provenance — built before the path opens, not after
What it gains you
  • Full pipeline speed for the change classes that qualify.
What it costs you
  • Engineered verification must be made a stronger check than a human in the path.
  • Observed as no team's ceiling — reached by narrowing what qualifies, never by widening who may act.

Fits a team that believes engineered verification can be made a stronger check than a human in the path — and that the honest route there is to narrow what qualifies, never to widen who may act.
