# Retire "graph engineering" in favour of "agent orchestration"

The commissioning brief for this project named "graph engineering" as one of the disciplines the
documents must cover, alongside context engineering and harness engineering. Research against
primary sources found it is not a term of art: no framework documentation, vendor engineering
blog, paper, standards body or named practitioner uses it, and LangGraph — the framework it would
most plausibly describe — calls itself "a low-level orchestration framework" instead. All located
usage traces to a 2026 content-marketing cluster citing itself. It additionally collides with the
long-established and unrelated "knowledge graph engineering". We therefore use **agent
orchestration** as the name of the discipline, and **flow engineering** for the narrower sense of
encoding control flow explicitly rather than leaving sequencing to the agent.

## Considered options

- **Use "graph engineering" as briefed.** Rejected: the documents are meant to be reference-grade
  and cited, and this term cannot be cited to anything. Publishing it would manufacture vocabulary
  under the appearance of reporting it.
- **Use it with a caveat noting it is nonstandard.** Rejected: it would still need defining from
  scratch in every document, and the caveat costs more words than simply using the real term.
- **Use "agent orchestration".** Chosen. Established, citable, and unambiguous.

## Consequences

The person who commissioned this work asked for the term and explicitly asked to be told the
correct one instead, so this is a fulfilment of the brief rather than a deviation from it. The
glossary page should say plainly that "graph engineering" is not established vocabulary — readers
arriving with the phrase in mind are better served by being told than by silence.
